\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning (RL) in massively parallel simulation has enabled agile legged locomotion and loco-manipulation on rigid terrain, yet its extension to deformable granular media such as sand, regolith, and snow remains limited.
Existing approaches either approximate robot-terrain interaction with reduced-order contact models that cannot represent displaced material and persistent deformation, or simulate particles within a bounded workspace whose cost grows with the simulated terrain volume.
In this work, we present a moving terrain patch for the material point method (MPM) that restricts material updates to the region surrounding the robot and its interacting objects.
Particle recycling supplies terrain entering the patch, while a particle cache stores the state of departing material and restores it when the robot revisits the region.
This enables parallel policy training over unbounded terrain with MPM coupled to articulated robots.
We further introduce Particle NeRD, which accelerates material updates within a restricted material-parameter range by predicting particle displacement with a collider-conditioned Transolver and reconstructing body reaction wrenches analytically.
We train policies for Franka particle pushing, as well as velocity tracking and box pushing with the Unitree G1 humanoid, on MPM terrain.

\end{abstract}
